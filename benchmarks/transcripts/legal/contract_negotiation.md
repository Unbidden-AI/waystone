# legal / contract_negotiation

Sarah: Hi Marcus, thanks for coming in. I've had a thorough look at the SaaS vendor contract from "CloudServe Inc." for their analytics platform.

Marcus: Hi Sarah, good to see you. Great, what are your initial thoughts?

Sarah: Overall, it's fairly standard, but there are definitely a few areas we need to address. Let's start with the Limitation of Liability clause, Section 8.2.

Marcus: Okay, what's the issue there?

Sarah: They've capped their liability at three months of fees paid by your company, "InnovateTech Solutions." Given your projected annual spend of $120,000, that's only $30,000.

Marcus: That seems incredibly low. If they have a major data breach or service outage that impacts our operations, $30,000 wouldn't even cover a fraction of our potential losses.

Sarah: Exactly. This is a critical redline for me. We need to push for a cap of at least twelve months of fees or $500,000, whichever is greater, and ideally carve out gross negligence and willful misconduct entirely.

Marcus: Agreed. That's a non-starter as is.

Sarah: Next, let's talk about data. The Data Processing Agreement, or DPA, attached as Schedule B, is a bit light. It doesn't adequately address sub-processor management or specific data breach notification timelines under GDPR Article 33 or CCPA requirements.

Marcus: Our platform handles a lot of sensitive customer data, so robust data protection is paramount. We can't afford any gaps there.

Sarah: Absolutely. This is another critical redline. We need to revise the DPA to be fully compliant with both GDPR and CCPA, including clear provisions for audit rights, sub-processor approval, and detailed incident response protocols.

Marcus: Got it. What else?

Sarah: Section 4.5, the auto-renewal clause. It states the contract automatically renews for successive one-year terms unless you provide 90 days' written notice prior to the end of the current term.

Marcus: Ninety days? That's a long window. We'd prefer something shorter, maybe 30 or 60 days. It gives us more flexibility if our needs change.

Sarah: I agree. A 90-day notice period can be burdensome. I'd classify this as a preferred change. We should aim for 60 days, which is a more common and reasonable standard.

Marcus: Okay, 60 days sounds much better.

Sarah: Then there's Section 7.1, regarding Intellectual Property. It has language that could be interpreted as CloudServe having some ownership rights over "customer data" generated through their platform.

Marcus: Whoa, absolutely not. All data our customers input, or that's generated from their use of our service, belongs to InnovateTech and our customers. That's non-negotiable.

Sarah: My thoughts exactly. This is our third critical redline. We need explicit language confirming that InnovateTech Solutions retains all rights, title, and interest in and to all customer data.

Marcus: Good. What about indemnification? Are we protected if they infringe on someone else's IP?

Sarah: Currently, Section 9.1 covers CloudServe indemnifying you for third-party claims arising from their IP infringement or breach of confidentiality. However, it's not mutual.

Marcus: So, we're on the hook if, say, one of our users uploads something that infringes on a third party, but they're not fully on the hook for their own platform's issues?

Sarah: Precisely. My second preferred change would be to make the indemnification mutual, so your company is also indemnified for claims arising from CloudServe's platform or services infringing on third-party IP.

Marcus: That makes sense. So, to recap, what are the absolute must-haves and the strong preferences?

Sarah: The three critical redlines are: increasing the Limitation of Liability cap, a fully compliant and robust DPA, and explicit confirmation of your IP ownership over customer data.

Marcus: And the preferences?

Sarah: The two preferred changes are reducing the auto-renewal notice period to 60 days and making the indemnification clause mutual.

Marcus: Okay, that's clear. How should we approach them with these?

Sarah: I'll draft a redlined version of the contract with a cover letter explaining our positions. We'll present the critical redlines as non-negotiable requirements for moving forward, and the preferred changes as points for discussion. We can expect some back-and-forth, but these are all reasonable requests.

Marcus: Sounds like a solid plan, Sarah. Thanks for your thorough review.

Sarah: My pleasure, Marcus. I'll send you the draft by end of day tomorrow.
