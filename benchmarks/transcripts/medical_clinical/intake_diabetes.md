Mr. Chen: Empagliflozin? Is that a new one? Will it have side effects?
Dr. Rivera: It's been around for a while and is very effective. Common side effects can include increased urination and, rarely, yeast infections. We'll discuss all the details and precautions.
Dr. Rivera: Beyond medication, I also want to refer you to our registered dietitian, Sarah Jenkins. She can help you develop a personalized meal plan that supports better glucose control.
Mr. Chen: A dietitian sounds like a good idea. I know my diet could be better.
Dr. Rivera: Excellent. And for monitoring, I'd like you to start checking your blood glucose more frequently. Let's aim for fasting every morning, and then two hours after breakfast and dinner, at least three days a week.
Mr. Chen: That's more often than I do now, but I can do that.
Dr. Rivera: Great. We'll also schedule a follow-up visit in about three months to recheck your A1C and see how you're responding to the new medication and dietary changes.
Mr. Chen: Okay, Dr. Rivera. That sounds like a plan. I'm ready to get this under control.
